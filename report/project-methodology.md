# Project Methodology
## Agile vs. Waterfall 
From FYP Supervisor meeting 01:   
   
*Add an Agile vs Waterfall paragraph comparing both, explaining the chosen approach and why Waterfall was not selected.*

---

I chose an agile methodology, modified Scrum, over the traditional waterfall approach because of the exploratory nature of my final year project. Waterfall assumes that requirements and scope are fixed from the start and that works flows sequentially. This was not possible with Aeroq due to unfixed academic requirements at the outset and my lack of experience with the technologies the project requires.

Agile assumes that requirements and scope will evolve as the project progresses and that work is delivered in short, iterative sprints with regular feedback. This allows the project to adapt as my understanding improves and aligns with the CRISP-DM, as well as providing a mechanism to get early feedback through producing increments that my supervisor, Cian, can review. Agile accommodates my fixed academic timeline while still allowing flexibility in scope; I can plan sprints to meet deadlines while using MoSCoW prioritisation to protect important deliverables. It also lowers overall project risk, because high-risk activities can be undertaken while there's still time to correct or pivot.
